Boundary conditions and limitations

This page answers the questions that the SciMLBase PDE interface asks every discretizer to document: the supported geometries and coordinates; the supported initial, Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, periodic and interface conditions; how conditions are validated and imposed; and the restrictions of the discretization.

Restrictions of this release:

  • Geometries are Ferrite.Grid meshes (1D, 2D and 3D cell shapes) wrapped as MeshDomain(grid), with boundary pieces named by the grid's facet sets. Only 1D and 2D are evidenced; meshes with mixed cell types are rejected until M4.
  • Coordinates are Cartesian and named by the (x, y) ∈ Ω pairing. Time must be the first argument of every dependent variable.
  • Dirichlet data may depend on space, time and parameters but not on another field. Neumann and Robin conditions are written with NormalDerivative(Γ), with any conormal coefficient written explicitly. Neumann and Robin data may depend on time; the Robin coefficient multiplying u(t, Γ) may depend only on space and parameters until M3.
  • Periodic conditions u(t, Γa) ~ u(t, Γb) are rejected until M4. Interface conditions between regions have no syntax; every field lives on the whole mesh.
  • Boundary pieces must be disjoint. A piece with no condition gets the natural zero-flux condition, with a warning when the operator has a flux.
  • Where two Dirichlet pieces of one field meet, the condition listed last in the PDESystem wins.
  • Every field with a time derivative needs an initial condition, linear in that one field and finite at every node.
  • Steady problems, dirichlet = :eliminate, fields with different interpolations and test spaces other than Galerkin() are rejected until M4. Nonlinear operators, time-dependent mass or stiffness coefficients and codegen = :mtk are rejected until M3.

"Time-dependent" means boundary data that varies with time, for every condition kind.

Geometries and coordinates

The spatial domain is a MeshDomain(grid) of a Ferrite.Grid, for example one read from Gmsh with FerriteGmsh.togrid or made with Ferrite.generate_grid. Boundary pieces are the grid's facet sets: boundarypiece(Ω, :inlet) is the facet set "inlet". Pieces must be disjoint; a facet in two pieces that both carry conditions is an error naming the first shared facet. Line, triangle and quadrilateral meshes are tested; tetrahedral and hexahedral meshes construct but are not evidenced (see Supported equation classes). Every cell of the mesh must have the same shape.

Coordinates are Cartesian. Their names and order come from the (x, y) ∈ Ω pairing (see Coordinate binding). A curvilinear operator, such as a radial Laplacian, is written out in Cartesian-coordinate form in the equation.

Initial conditions

An initial condition is written as for MethodOfLines, u(0, x, y) ~ f(x, y), at the start of the time interval. It must give the value of one dependent variable, be linear in it, and be finite at every node of the mesh; a non-finite value is an error naming the node. Every field with a time derivative needs one. A field without a time derivative, such as v in 0 ~ Dx(Dx(v(t, x, y))) + …, is algebraic: it needs no initial condition, and its equation must be paired with it through FEMDiscretization(…; pairing = Dict(eq => v)) when the pairing cannot be inferred.

Dirichlet conditions

u(t, Γ) ~ g with g constant or a function of the coordinates, time and parameters:

u(t, Γ_in) ~ 1
u(t, Γ_in) ~ sin(t)
u(t, Γ_in) ~ (1 + x + 2y) * (1 + t)

Each Dirichlet node becomes an algebraic row u_d = g_d(t) of the DAEProblem, so the condition is imposed exactly at the nodes, not weakly. Time-dependent data is evaluated at the current time of every residual evaluation. The data may not depend on another field: u(t, Γ) + v(t, Γ) ~ 1 is an error.

With default solver settings these rows hold to about the solver tolerance; see "Exact Dirichlet rows" in Getting started for the setting that holds them to round-off.

Corners. Where two pieces with Dirichlet conditions on the same field meet, the shared nodes take the condition listed last in the PDESystem's boundary conditions, as in Ferrite's ConstraintHandler. Listing a Dirichlet condition on the same piece and field twice is an error.

Neumann conditions

Dn(u(t, Γ)) ~ h with Dn = NormalDerivative(Γ), the derivative along the outward unit normal of Γ. The data h may depend on the coordinates, time and parameters. The condition enters the weak form as a facet integral, so it holds weakly, not node by node.

When the equation's flux has a coefficient, write it: for Dt(u) ~ Dx(k * Dx(u)) + … the natural condition is on k * Dn(u(t, Γ)). A condition written against the wrong coefficient is rescaled when it is proportional to the conormal derivative; a condition that is not, such as Dx(u(t, Γ)) ~ h on a piece that is not perpendicular to the x axis, is an error asking for the normal derivative.

A boundary piece with no condition gets the natural zero-flux condition. When the equations have a flux, the discretization warns about each such piece.

Robin conditions

k * Dn(u(t, Γ)) + α * u(t, Γ) ~ β, with the conormal coefficient written explicitly as for Neumann conditions. The data β may depend on the coordinates, time and parameters. The coefficient α may depend on the coordinates and parameters but not on time in this release: a time-dependent α makes the boundary part of the stiffness operator time-dependent, which is rejected with time-dependent coefficients in the mass or stiffness operator land in M3. Like a Neumann condition, it enters the weak form as a facet integral.

Periodic conditions

Not supported in this release. An equation between traces on two pieces, u(t, Γ_left) ~ u(t, Γ_right), is rejected with periodic conditions need milestone M4 (Ferrite PeriodicDirichlet).

Interface conditions

Not supported. There is no syntax for a condition on an interior interface between two regions, and every field is defined on the whole mesh. Discontinuous coefficients across an interface are not evidenced (see Supported equation classes).

Validation

Every condition is checked when the system is discretized, by DAEProblem(pdesys, disc) or discretize(pdesys, disc), before a solver runs. An unsupported condition, operator or option is an error that names the problem; nothing falls back to mtkcompile.
